Looking after your garden when you're away for the winter comes down to three things: leave the irrigation on but reduced to winter settings, do the pruning and tidying before you go, and arrange for someone to check the garden every few weeks. On the Costa Blanca winter is a growing season, not a dormant one, so a garden left completely alone for four months will need work when you return.

## Why a Mediterranean garden can't just be shut for the winter

A Mediterranean garden keeps growing in winter. Unlike in northern Europe, where the garden sleeps until spring, here the autumn rains and mild temperatures wake everything up: lawns, hedges, weeds and fruit trees all carry on.

For a villa in Moraira (Alicante) or anywhere in the Marina Alta, that has practical consequences. Weeds that sprout with the first rains can be knee-high by February. Rain is irregular: there can be weeks of storms followed by a long dry spell, so plants still depend on irrigation. And winter brings strong easterly storms, known locally as a *temporal de levante*, which can bring down loose fronds and branches.

None of this is a reason to worry. It just means the garden needs a plan rather than a lock on the gate.

## Looking after your garden when you're away for the winter: the checklist before you leave

The week before you leave is when most of the work gets done, ideally in this order:

1. **Set the irrigation timer to winter mode.** Reduce the frequency rather than switching it off (more on this below) and replace the timer battery, even if it still works.
2. **Check the drippers and sprinklers.** Run each zone for a few minutes and look for leaks, blocked drippers and sprinklers pointing at walls. Hard water in this area clogs drippers with lime over time.
3. **Prune what overhangs.** Branches touching the roof, the gutters, the pool fence or a neighbour's wall are the ones that cause trouble in a storm.
4. **Remove dead palm fronds.** A dry frond hanging down is the first thing a strong wind brings down.
5. **Secure or move pots and furniture.** Group pots against a sheltered wall and store cushions, umbrellas and light chairs.
6. **Clear drains, gutters and grilles.** Leaves blocking a drain are the most common cause of flooded terraces after heavy rain.
7. **Cut the lawn and trim hedges.** Leaving them short gives you a few extra weeks before they look overgrown.
8. **Pick ripe fruit.** Oranges, lemons and mandarins ripen in winter; fruit left to rot on the ground attracts flies and pests.

## How to set the irrigation timer for winter

In winter the irrigation should be reduced, not switched off. Most Mediterranean shrubs and established trees need much less water from November to February, but a dry month without any irrigation can still damage young plants, pots and lawn.

As a general guide for a garden on the coast:

| Part of the garden | Summer | Winter while you're away |
|---|---|---|
| Lawn | Most days | Once or twice a week, or off if it has rained a lot |
| Hedges and beds on drip | Every 2-3 days | Once a week |
| Established Mediterranean shrubs | Once or twice a week | Every 2-3 weeks, or only in dry spells |
| Pots on drip | Daily | Every 3-4 days |
| Recently planted trees and shrubs | Every 2-3 days | Once a week |

These are starting points, not fixed rules: soil, shade and the type of plant all change the answer. If your timer has a rain sensor, check it is connected. If it does not, the person checking the garden should be able to switch the irrigation off after a big storm and back on again when the soil dries.

A power cut can reset some older timers to factory settings, which can mean no watering at all or summer schedules in January. It is worth asking whoever checks the garden to look at the timer screen on every visit. ## What can wait until you're back, and what can't

Some jobs can safely wait for your return; others get worse every week.

**Can wait:**

- Planting new shrubs and flowers (although autumn and winter are the best planting season here, so there is no harm in doing it before you go).
- Feeding the garden.
- Reshaping or redesigning beds.

**Should not wait:**

- **Palm pruning.** Winter, from December to February, is the right time to prune palms, because the red palm weevil is much less active in the cold. If you are away all winter, arrange it rather than leaving it until spring.
- **Pine processionary nests.** The pine processionary caterpillar builds white, silky nests on pine branches in winter. Its hairs can cause serious reactions in dogs, children and adults, so nests near paths, play areas or windows should be removed by a professional before the caterpillars come down in late winter.
- **Damage after a storm.** A broken branch resting on a roof or a fence will not get better on its own.

## Frequently asked questions

### Should I turn off the irrigation when I leave for the winter?

No, it is better to reduce it than to switch it off. Winter rain on the Costa Blanca is irregular, and a dry month can damage pots, lawn and recently planted shrubs. Set a winter schedule and make sure someone can switch it off after heavy rain.

### How long can a garden go without anyone checking it in winter?

Most gardens on the coast cope well with a visit every two to four weeks in winter. Gardens with lawn, many pots or young plants need more frequent checks than gravel gardens with established Mediterranean shrubs.

### What should I do with potted plants when I go away?

Group them together against a sheltered wall, out of the strongest wind, and connect them to the drip system if possible. Remove saucers in winter so roots do not sit in water after rain.

### Is there a risk of frost in winter on the Costa Blanca?

Frost is rare on the coast, but it can happen some nights in inland valleys such as Xaló. If you have frost-sensitive plants away from the coast, place pots in a sheltered spot and ask whoever checks the garden to cover them if a cold night is forecast.
